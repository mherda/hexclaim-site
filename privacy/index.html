Hexclaim privacy policy

Last updated 3 November 2026

  • Your walks, routes and claimed hexes stay on your iPhone and in your own iCloud, which we can't read.
  • Only if you use friend groups do we store anything about you: an account id, the names you type, and which hexes you claimed each week, without times or routes.
  • No ads, no tracking, no analytics, and we never sell data.
  • You can delete your account, and everything we hold about you, in the app.

Who we are

Hexclaim is made by Marcin Herda, an independent developer in the United Kingdom, who is responsible for the data described here.

What stays on your iPhone and in your iCloud

To claim hexes, Hexclaim uses your location while you're on a walk. Your location trail, walks, claimed hexes, places you've found, progress, badges and privacy zones are stored on your iPhone and synced to your other devices through your private iCloud account. We have no access to this data.

  • Apple Health: if you turn on Save walks to Apple Health in Settings, each walk you finish in Hexclaim is saved to Health on your iPhone as an outdoor walk, with its time, distance, steps and route. Hexclaim doesn't read anything from Health, and we never see your Health data. Walks imported from Health by earlier versions stay in Hexclaim.
  • Motion and fitness: used on your iPhone to tell walking apart from cycling or driving. It isn't sent anywhere.
  • Photos: Hexclaim only saves the share cards you choose to save. It doesn't read your photo library.

Friend groups

Friend groups are optional. If you use them, we store the following with our database provider, Supabase, in the European Union (Ireland):

  • An account id. You sign in with Apple. We don't ask Apple for your name or email address, so we don't receive them.
  • The names you type: your display name in a group, group names and co-op goals.
  • Your hexes for each week: the IDs of the map hexes (each about 50 metres across) that you claimed that week. Hexes inside your privacy zones are never sent. We don't store times, routes, walks or your current location.
  • A push token, while you're in a group: it lets Apple's push notification service wake Hexclaim when a friend has walked, so it can fetch the update and tell you. The push itself carries no names or hexes. The token is deleted when you leave your last group, sign out or delete your account.

The other members of a group can see your display name and your hexes in that group. We use this data only to run friend groups, which is the service you ask for when you join one.

Maps and places

To work out which area you're in and which places are nearby, Hexclaim sends a position rounded to about 100 metres to OpenStreetMap's public map servers (Overpass and Nominatim), once for each new area. No account id or other identifier is sent with it. When you ask for a suggested walk, Apple Maps provides the walking directions.

Invite links and this website

This website and its invite links are hosted by Vercel. Like most web hosts, Vercel keeps standard server logs, such as IP addresses, for a limited time. An invite link contains the group's invite code, which the page shows so you can type it into the app.

What we don't do

Hexclaim has no advertising, no third-party analytics or tracking code, and doesn't combine your data with data from other companies. We don't sell or share your data with anyone except the providers named above, who process it on our behalf.

How long we keep it

  • Group data stays until it's removed. Leaving a group, or being removed from it, deletes your hexes in that group. Deleting a group deletes everything in it.
  • Settings › Account › Delete account deletes your account and everything we hold about you on our server straight away. It also signs Hexclaim out of your Apple ID, so it no longer appears under Sign in with Apple.
  • Data on your iPhone is removed when you delete the app. Your iCloud copy can be managed in your iPhone's iCloud settings.

Your rights

Under UK and EU data protection law, you can ask to see, correct or delete your data, and object to how it's used. You can delete everything yourself in the app at any time. You can also complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk) or your local data protection authority.
